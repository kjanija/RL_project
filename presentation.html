Introduction

The game: Order and Chaos

  • 6×6 board, two players: Order moves first, then they alternate.
  • On each turn a player picks an empty square and a symbol, X or O: both players use both symbols.
  • Order wins with at least 5 identical symbols in a row (horizontal, vertical, diagonal).
  • Chaos wins if the board fills up with no such line.
In the environment a line of 6 also counts as a win, and if Chaos is the one to complete a line of 5, Order wins (rules of the original game).

Reward

A theoretical reference exists

The paper Order versus Chaos (IEEE CoG 2020) gives a strategy with which Order wins the original game. We can therefore compare what the agent learns with a known winning strategy.

Research question

Can a DQN agent learn to win as Order against an increasingly defensive Chaos, and how close does it get to the theoretical strategy?

Theory · the paper

“Order versus Chaos”: how Order wins

van den Bergh, Castelein, van Gent — IEEE CoG 2020, pp. 391–398

  • Border (20 squares, 10 opposite pairs): if Chaos plays on the border, Order answers on the paired square with the opposite symbol.
  • 4×4 centre: Order plays a strategy that forces a homogeneous line of 4 (Lemma IV.3).
  • Closing: that line of 4 plus the aligned border pair gives 5 in a row.
  • Inductive step (Lemma IV.4): if Order wins the n×n case with line n, it also wins (n+2)×(n+2) with line n+1.
4×4 centre border mirroring pairs
Theory · formulation

Theory · from the model to samples

Approximate value iteration → DQN

Value iteration (known model)

Qk+1 = T Qk   with   (TQ)(s,a) = E[ r + γ maxa′ Q(s′,a′) ]

T is a contraction in the ∞-norm with coefficient γ: Qk → Q*.

With 10¹⁷ states a table is impossible. Two things are replaced:

  • the sum over s′ with samples (s, a, r, s′);
  • the table with a network Qθ and an L² projection onto the replay-buffer data.

DQN loss (MSBE)

L(θ) = E(s,a,r,s′)∼D [ ( y − Qθ(s,a) )² ]
y = r + γ (1 − done) · maxa′ legal Qθ⁻(s′,a′)

y is treated as a constant (semi-gradient). θ⁻ are the weights of the target network.

Q-learning + network + bootstrapping is the “deadly triad” (Sutton & Barto): the replay buffer and the target network serve to stabilise it.
Theory · stability and bias

Replay, target network and Double DQN

Experience replay

Transitions stored in a buffer; uniform mini-batches. Breaks the correlation between samples and reuses data.

Target network

Delayed copy of the weights for the target: the target does not move at every step.

ε-greedy

With probability ε a random move among the legal ones, otherwise the greedy one. ε decays per stage.

DQN

y = r + γ · maxa′ Qθ⁻(s′,a′)
The same max chooses and evaluates the action: with noisy estimates E[max] ≥ max E → overestimation.

Double DQN (van Hasselt et al., 2016)

y = r + γ · Qθ⁻( s′, argmaxa′ Qθ(s′,a′) )
The online network chooses, the target evaluates. Almost no extra cost.
Theory · illegal actions

Invalid action masking

Baseline: penalise

Move on an occupied square → −1 and end of episode.

In the early phases most samples are wasted on illegal moves and the agent never reaches mid-game positions.

Project choice: mask

The environment exposes action_masks() (72 booleans).

Illegal actions are never chosen: 100% of the samples are valid transitions.

In Q-learning the mask is applied at two points

a = argmaxa ∈ A(s) Qθ(s,a)
action choice (also ε-greedy)
y = r + γ · maxa′ ∈ A(s′) Qθ⁻(s′,a′)
target: every transition stores next_mask
Implementation: illegal Q-values are set to −10⁹ (not −∞) to avoid 0·∞ = NaN. For policy-gradient methods the analogue is zeroing the illegal logits before the softmax (Huang & Ontañón).

Reward shaping and curriculum

Reward shaping

With a sparse reward and 10¹⁷ states exploration rarely finds a win: intermediate signals are added.

Caution

Only potential-based shaping preserves optimal policies (Ng et al., 1999). Our terms are not: they are small compared with ±1, but an ablation would be needed to rule out side effects.

Normalisation to [−1, 1]

With ±100 rewards the loss (MSBE) exploded: with bounded targets the gradients stay bounded too.
Cluster data: Job 1742771 (±100) peaked at 441,858 MSBE loss; reference run ([−1, 1]) stayed bounded at ≤ 0.0022 (> 10⁸× reduction).

Curriculum (Bengio et al., 2009)

Training on progressively harder tasks: here the difficulty is Chaos' strength, set by β.

Implementation · opponent and teacher

Heuristic Chaos and the teacher

Chaos (part of the environment)

Random: uniform among the moves that do not complete a line of 5.

Heuristic (with probability β, otherwise random):

  • blocks a 4-in-a-row with the opposite symbol;
  • spoils the 3-in-a-rows that are still open;
  • otherwise falls back to random.

With β = 1 it is practically deterministic: an agent could memorise a line of play.

Teacher (src/strategy.py)

  • Immediate winning move if one exists.
  • Extension of an inner line of 4 towards the border.
  • Mirror on the 10 border pairs (opposite symbol).
  • 4×4 centre: the paper's rules + 6 exceptions (Fig. 4) with D4 symmetries and X/O swap.

Used for: reward bonus (+0.02), final benchmark, “always wins” reference.

On the analysis branch the 4×4 centre uses an exact solver instead of the rules (see slide 23).

Why normalisation worked

With rewards in [−1, 1] and γ = 0.90, the optimal Q-values are bounded by |Q*| ≤ R_max / (1 − γ) = 10. The Bellman error cannot blow up.

Results · critical reading

How far to trust these numbers

Statistical uncertainty

With 100 games the standard error of an 83% is ≈ 3.8 points: the 95% interval is about [75%, 91%]. With 40 games (training evaluation) it is ≈ 6–7 points.

Selection bias

The best stage-2D checkpoint scored 92.5% in the evaluation but 83% in the independent hold-out: picking the maximum of noisy evaluations overestimates.

One run, one seed

Run-to-run variability cannot be estimated, nor can DQN and Double DQN be compared.

Known, deterministic opponent

Evaluated only against the training Chaos opponents; at β = 1 the heuristic is almost deterministic. Generalisation is not demonstrated.
Recent development · branch analysis

The teacher did not always win

98.9%
wins of the teacher against the heuristic Chaos (989/1000), despite being the paper's “always winning” strategy
16 / 21
losses (out of 1500 games) in the branch where the 4×4 centre was full with no line of 4 and the teacher had to open a border pair
2000/2000
wins after the fix, against both the heuristic and the random Chaos
  • Diagnosis: an exhaustive search on the 4×4 game found a Chaos sequence that fills the centre with no homogeneous line: our transcription of the paper's rules does not force the line.
  • An exact solver (minimax with memoisation) confirms that Order wins the 4×4.
  • Fix: precomputed table of 112,550 winning positions (0.45 MB), exploiting the 16 symmetries. The teacher plays the paper's move if it is winning, otherwise a winning move from the table.
  • Effect: the teacher reward bonus changes, so the old results are indicative.
Recent development · branch analysis

A more rigorous pipeline for the new runs

  • Seeds (--seed): Python, NumPy, PyTorch and environment resets.
  • Fixed-seed evaluation on a separate environment: checkpoints are compared on the same games.
  • 100 evaluation games (previously 40); at equal win rate the checkpoint with fewer moves wins.
  • Stage hand-over with the best checkpoint, documented.
  • Separate outputs: models/<algorithm>_seed<N>/.
  • Determinism tests and a 4×4 verification; CLUSTER_GUIDE.md guide.

Limitations and next steps

What is missing

Current limitations

  • Chaos does not learn: no self-play, no guarantee against an optimal Chaos.
  • Teacher bonus and shaping not ablated.
  • No unseen opponents in the evaluation.
  • Reference results from a single seed.

Possible developments

  • Ablations: teacher on/off, shaping on/off, curriculum vs direct training at β = 1.
  • Generalisation: other heuristics, MCTS, perturbed Chaos.
  • Efficiency: data augmentation with the game's symmetries (up to 16×), n-step, dueling, prioritised replay.
  • Self-play AlphaZero-style (network + MCTS).
Conclusions

What we achieved

Results

  • A DQN agent with action masking wins 100% against random Chaos and 83% against the full blocker, with no illegal moves.
  • The 5-stage curriculum and buffer trimming stabilise training.
  • The teacher was verified and fixed: it now wins 2000/2000.

Lessons

  • Normalising rewards matters as much as the algorithm.
  • Evaluate on independent games: the maximum of noisy evaluations is optimistic.
  • Even a strategy “from the paper” must be verified: the exhaustive check found the flaw.
Next step: DQN vs Double DQN on 3 seeds with the rigorous pipeline, then ablations and unseen opponents.
